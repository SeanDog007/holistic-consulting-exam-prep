Part 1: NANP Code of Ethics (Q1–20)
1. According to the NANP Code of Ethics, the responsibility of an NANP member extends beyond the individual client to which of the following?
A) The member's employer only
B) The NANP Board of Directors only
C) Society
D) Dietary supplement manufacturers
Correct Answer: C) Society
Rationale: The Code's honored ideals state that a member's responsibility reaches past the individual client to society as a whole. This is why members are also expected to support the nutritional well-being of the community.
Reference: NANP, Code of Ethics (introduction)
2. A graduate of a holistic nutrition program adds "BCHN" after her name on her website, but NANP has not approved her to use it. Under the Code of Ethics, this is:
A) Acceptable, because she graduated from an NANP-approved program
B) A violation, because BCHN® may only be used with NANP's express written permission and approval
C) Acceptable in states with no nutrition licensure law
D) Acceptable if she also names her school
Correct Answer: B) A violation, because BCHN® may only be used with NANP's express written permission and approval
Rationale: The Code bars members from using Board Certified in Holistic Nutrition®, BCHN®, Certified Dietary Supplement Professional™ or CDSP™ without NANP's express written permission and approval. Graduation alone does not confer the credential.
Reference: NANP, Code of Ethics (credentials principle)
3. A member wants to stop working with a client who repeatedly misses sessions. According to the Code of Ethics, the member may:
A) End services immediately without telling the client
B) Never end services once they have begun
C) End services only with NANP's permission
D) Discontinue service only after giving the client due notice
Correct Answer: D) Discontinue service only after giving the client due notice
Rationale: Once a member undertakes a client, the member may not neglect that client unless discharged. Service may be discontinued, but only after due notice is given to the client.
Reference: NANP, Code of Ethics (client relationship principle)
4. A practitioner declines a prospective client whose goals fall outside the practitioner's area of focus. Under the Code of Ethics, this decision is:
A) Permissible, because members may choose whom they will serve
B) A violation of the duty to improve community nutritional well-being
C) Permissible only if the person is referred to a dietitian
D) A violation unless NANP is notified
Correct Answer: A) Permissible, because members may choose whom they will serve
Rationale: The Code explicitly allows members to choose whom they serve. The duty not to neglect a client applies only after the member has undertaken that client.
Reference: NANP, Code of Ethics (client relationship principle)
5. A client presents with several overlapping symptoms the practitioner does not fully understand. The Code of Ethics directs the member to:
A) Continue the standard protocol and monitor
B) Discharge the client immediately
C) Seek consultation, because the case is doubtful or complex and other professionals' services appear warranted
D) Research the symptoms online and proceed independently
Correct Answer: C) Seek consultation, because the case is doubtful or complex and other professionals' services appear warranted
Rationale: The Code requires members to seek consultation in doubtful or complex cases and whenever other professionals could provide more comprehensive or higher-quality advice.
Reference: NANP, Code of Ethics (consultation principle)
6. Under the NANP Code of Ethics, when may a member reveal information a client shared in confidence during a consultation?
A) When a family member asks
B) When required to do so by law
C) When posting a de-identified case study on social media
D) Whenever the member believes it would help the client
Correct Answer: B) When required to do so by law
Rationale: Members will not reveal confidences entrusted during consultations unless the law requires it. Good intentions or family requests are not exceptions.
Reference: NANP, Code of Ethics (confidentiality principle)
7. The Code of Ethics states that a member will not exceed their scope of service or practice. Which two limits does the Code name?
A) Income and client volume
B) Education and years of experience
C) State and federal tax status
D) Abilities and law
Correct Answer: D) Abilities and law
Rationale: The Code says members will not exceed their scope of service or practice either in abilities or by law. Both personal competence and legal limits define scope.
Reference: NANP, Code of Ethics (scope principle)
8. A colleague invites an NANP member to join a practice that operates in violation of state law. Under the Code of Ethics, the member should:
A) Decline, because members will not voluntarily associate or work with anyone who violates the principle that services be founded on a legal basis
B) Accept but keep separate client records
C) Accept if the colleague is also an NANP member
D) Accept, then report the colleague later
Correct Answer: A) Decline, because members will not voluntarily associate or work with anyone who violates the principle that services be founded on a legal basis
Rationale: Services must rest on a legal and practical basis under state and federal law. The Code adds that members will not voluntarily associate or work with anyone who violates this principle.
Reference: NANP, Code of Ethics (legal basis principle)
9. A member completes continuing education and shares what she learned with clients and colleagues. Which Code of Ethics principle does this most directly fulfill?
A) Confidentiality
B) Freedom of speech
C) Continually striving to improve skills and knowledge and making professional attainments available to clients and colleagues
D) Choosing whom to serve
Correct Answer: C) Continually striving to improve skills and knowledge and making professional attainments available to clients and colleagues
Rationale: The Code commits members to ongoing skill and knowledge development and to sharing those attainments with the people they serve and work alongside.
Reference: NANP, Code of Ethics (professional development principle)
10. An NANP member learns that a fellow member is advising clients in a clearly illegal way. The Code of Ethics directs members to:
A) Ignore it to preserve collegial relationships
B) Oppose the illegal or unethical conduct of fellow members without hesitation
C) Wait until a client files a complaint
D) Discuss it only with clients they share
Correct Answer: B) Oppose the illegal or unethical conduct of fellow members without hesitation
Rationale: Members commit to protecting the public from counselors lacking moral character or professional competence, to accepting the profession's self-imposed disciplines, and to opposing illegal or unethical conduct by fellow members.
Reference: NANP, Code of Ethics (protecting the public principle)
11. Under the AI Policy in the NANP Code of Ethics, who is responsible for the accuracy, integrity and independence of client materials prepared with an AI tool?
A) The AI tool's vendor
B) NANP
C) The client who receives the materials
D) The NANP member
Correct Answer: D) The NANP member
Rationale: Under the Human Accountability provision, the member keeps full responsibility for content given to clients or submitted in educational settings. AI may not replace qualified subject matter experts in deciding content.
Reference: NANP, Code of Ethics, AI Policy (Human Accountability)
12. A practitioner uses an AI tool to draft a client handout. Per the NANP AI Policy, what must the handout include?
A) A disclaimer disclosing AI use to the reader
B) A list of the AI tool's training sources
C) A signature from a licensed provider
D) Nothing, as long as the member reviewed it
Correct Answer: A) A disclaimer disclosing AI use to the reader
Rationale: The AI Policy's transparency standard requires that AI use be disclosed to the reader, and it states that a disclaimer is required. Review by the member does not remove this requirement.
Reference: NANP, Code of Ethics, AI Policy (Transparency)
13. Which practice is prohibited by the privacy and security section of the NANP AI Policy?
A) Using AI to brainstorm general recipe ideas
B) Using an institution-approved AI system that meets privacy requirements
C) Entering a client's sensitive data into a public or consumer AI tool without adequate data privacy protections
D) Disclosing AI use to readers
Correct Answer: C) Entering a client's sensitive data into a public or consumer AI tool without adequate data privacy protections
Rationale: The policy states that learner, client and other sensitive data must not go into public or consumer AI tools lacking adequate privacy protections. All AI use must also follow applicable privacy, security, legal and institutional policies.
Reference: NANP, Code of Ethics, AI Policy (Privacy and Security)
14. A student cites a chatbot's answer as the evidence base for a case study. This conflicts with which statement in the NANP AI Policy?
A) AI must be disclosed only in clinical settings
B) AI is not primary research and does not replace the best available research evidence
C) AI may replace subject matter experts once verified
D) AI may be used only for marketing materials
Correct Answer: B) AI is not primary research and does not replace the best available research evidence
Rationale: The policy is explicit that AI output is not primary research. Practitioners must still rely on the best available research evidence.
Reference: NANP, Code of Ethics, AI Policy (Human Accountability)
15. Why does the NANP AI Policy require authors to evaluate AI output before using it?
A) AI output is automatically owned by NANP
B) AI tools cannot produce nutrition content
C) Clients must approve all AI-generated content
D) AI tools are sometimes trained on biased and outdated information, so the author must catch bias and errors
Correct Answer: D) AI tools are sometimes trained on biased and outdated information, so the author must catch bias and errors
Rationale: The policy notes that AI training data can be biased or outdated. The author is responsible for evaluating output to reduce bias and errors.
Reference: NANP, Code of Ethics, AI Policy (Human Accountability)
16. According to the NANP AI Policy, using copyrighted material that appears in AI output:
A) Requires permission
B) Is allowed because the AI generated it
C) Is allowed without limit for educational use
D) Requires only a footnote
Correct Answer: A) Requires permission
Rationale: AI tools are often trained on publicly available information that includes copyrighted works. The policy states that using copyrighted materials requires permission.
Reference: NANP, Code of Ethics, AI Policy (Human Accountability)
17. The NANP Code of Ethics commits members to vigorously defending which right?
A) The Second Amendment right to bear arms
B) The First Amendment right to freedom of speech and press to impart truthful information about diet and nutrition
C) The Fourth Amendment protection against unreasonable searches
D) The Tenth Amendment reservation of powers to the states
Correct Answer: B) The First Amendment right to freedom of speech and press to impart truthful information about diet and nutrition
Rationale: The Code's closing principle commits members to defending First Amendment free speech and press rights to share truthful diet and nutrition information.
Reference: NANP, Code of Ethics (closing principle)
18. The Code of Ethics also pledges members to protect the public's "health freedom" right. In the Code, this means:
A) The right to free health care
B) The right to refuse any medical treatment
C) The public's right to obtain diet and nutrition information from the sources they choose
D) The right to buy dietary supplements tax-free
Correct Answer: C) The public's right to obtain diet and nutrition information from the sources they choose
Rationale: The Code pairs free speech with the public's health freedom right to get nutrition information from sources of their own choosing.
Reference: NANP, Code of Ethics (closing principle)
19. The NANP Code of Ethics is described as standards for judging the propriety of conduct in relationships with which groups?
A) Clients, colleagues, members of allied professions and the public
B) Clients and supplement suppliers only
C) Insurance companies and state boards
D) Employers and students only
Correct Answer: A) Clients, colleagues, members of allied professions and the public
Rationale: The Code's introduction names all four relationships. It is meant to guide members individually and collectively toward a high standard of ethical conduct.
Reference: NANP, Code of Ethics (introduction)
20. Which of the following would violate the credentials principle of the NANP Code of Ethics?
A) Listing a completed degree accurately on a website
B) Stating that you are currently a student in a program
C) Displaying CDSP™ after receiving NANP's written approval
D) Intentionally implying you hold a graduate degree you did not complete
Correct Answer: D) Intentionally implying you hold a graduate degree you did not complete
Rationale: Members may not intentionally misrepresent their education or professional credentials. Accurate statements and NANP-approved credential use are permitted.
Reference: NANP, Code of Ethics (credentials principle)
Part 2: NANP Legislative Affairs (Q21–36)
21. According to NANP Legislative Affairs, in how many U.S. states are Holistic Nutrition Professionals (HNPs) licensed?
A) 13
B) 5
C) None
D) All 50
Correct Answer: C) None
Rationale: NANP states plainly that HNPs are not licensed in any state. Some states license dietitians or nutritionists, but no state issues a holistic nutrition license.
Reference: NANP, Legislative Affairs (Scope of Practice)
22. Under the NANP Scope of Practice, which of the following do HNPs NOT do?
A) Diagnose, treat, cure, prevent, prescribe, heal or manage any disease state
B) Teach classes on healthy eating
C) Provide general nutrition information
D) Recommend whole, unprocessed foods
Correct Answer: A) Diagnose, treat, cure, prevent, prescribe, heal or manage any disease state
Rationale: The NANP Scope of Practice lists all seven of these verbs as outside the HNP role. Education about food and general nutrition stays within scope.
Reference: NANP, Legislative Affairs (Scope of Practice)
23. NANP defines Medical Nutrition Therapy (MNT), which HNPs do not practice, as:
A) Any individualized meal plan
B) Recommending supplements for general wellness
C) Group nutrition education
D) The provision of nutrition care services for the treatment or management of a disease or medical condition
Correct Answer: D) The provision of nutrition care services for the treatment or management of a disease or medical condition
Rationale: NANP uses this definition and notes that the legal definition of MNT may differ from state to state. Practitioners should review their own state's law.
Reference: NANP, Legislative Affairs (Scope of Practice)
24. NANP sorts U.S. states into three legislative classifications. Which list is correct?
A) Licensed, Certified and Registered States
B) Exclusive Scope of Practice, Title Protection and No Law or Certification States
C) Restricted, Semi-Restricted and Open States
D) Medical, Dietetic and Wellness States
Correct Answer: B) Exclusive Scope of Practice, Title Protection and No Law or Certification States
Rationale: These three categories determine how freely an HNP can practice in a given state. NANP's state map uses them to classify each state.
Reference: NANP, Legislative Affairs (state classifications)
25. Which state classification does NANP describe as the most difficult and restrictive for holistic nutrition professionals?
A) Exclusive Scope of Practice States
B) Title Protection States
C) No Law or Certification States
D) Compact Privilege States
Correct Answer: A) Exclusive Scope of Practice States
Rationale: These states require a license to provide nutrition care services. HNPs cannot obtain that license, so they rely on narrow exemptions.
Reference: NANP, Legislative Affairs (Exclusive Scope of Practice States)
26. In Exclusive Scope of Practice States, who may obtain a license to provide nutrition care services?
A) Anyone who passes the BCHN® exam
B) Any graduate of an NANP-approved program
C) Dietitians and, in some cases, Certified Nutrition Specialists
D) Health coaches with a national certification
Correct Answer: C) Dietitians and, in some cases, Certified Nutrition Specialists
Rationale: NANP explains that only dietitians, and sometimes Certified Nutrition Specialists (nutritionists), can be licensed in these states. Board certification in holistic nutrition is not a state license.
Reference: NANP, Legislative Affairs (Exclusive Scope of Practice States)
27. Exemption language in Exclusive Scope of Practice States typically allows HNPs to:
A) Provide individualized meal plans if clients sign a waiver
B) Assess nutrient status using lab work
C) Practice freely if they hold BCHN®
D) Provide general nutrition information, but not individualize dietary recommendations or assess a client's nutrient status
Correct Answer: D) Provide general nutrition information, but not individualize dietary recommendations or assess a client's nutrient status
Rationale: The exemption covers general information only. Individualizing recommendations and assessing nutrient status remain reserved for licensees in these states.
Reference: NANP, Legislative Affairs (Exclusive Scope of Practice States)
28. How does NANP describe "general nutrition information"?
A) Information tailored to one client's lab results
B) Information that applies to the masses and can be found in a book, medical journal, on the internet and similar sources
C) Any information given in a group setting
D) Information approved by a state licensing board
Correct Answer: B) Information that applies to the masses and can be found in a book, medical journal, on the internet and similar sources
Rationale: General nutrition information is not clearly defined in most laws, but NANP describes it as guidance meant for the general public. Podcasts and websites are also given as examples.
Reference: NANP, Legislative Affairs (A Note About General Nutrition Information)
29. An HNP practices in an Exclusive Scope of Practice State. Which activity is permitted under the general nutrition information exemption?
A) Sharing favorite recipes, cookbooks and blogs with a client
B) Writing an individualized meal plan for the client
C) Assessing the client's nutrient status
D) Using the title "nutritionist" on business cards
Correct Answer: A) Sharing favorite recipes, cookbooks and blogs with a client
Rationale: NANP lists favorite recipes, cookbooks, blogs, local farmers and meal delivery services as acceptable general resources. Individualized meal plans, nutrient-status assessment and protected titles are not allowed in these states.
Reference: NANP, Legislative Affairs (A Note About General Nutrition Information)
30. In a Title Protection State, which statement is accurate for an HNP?
A) The HNP may not work with individual clients at all
B) The HNP may use the title "dietitian" if board certified
C) The HNP may work with individuals and groups but may not use or imply the title dietitian, often nutritionist, or state licensure or certification
D) The HNP must be supervised by a licensed dietitian
Correct Answer: C) The HNP may work with individuals and groups but may not use or imply the title dietitian, often nutritionist, or state licensure or certification
Rationale: As the name suggests, these states protect titles rather than restricting services. In many of them "nutritionist" is also protected.
Reference: NANP, Legislative Affairs (Title Protection States)
31. Which state does NANP give as an example of a No Law or Certification State?
A) Alaska
B) Louisiana
C) New Mexico
D) Colorado
Correct Answer: D) Colorado
Rationale: NANP lists California, Washington State, Colorado, Arizona and Maine as examples with no regulatory limits on who may give nutrition advice. Some of these states still protect certain titles, so checking state law remains necessary.
Reference: NANP, Legislative Affairs (No Law or Certification States)
32. What legal penalties does NANP warn may apply to practitioners found guilty of practicing outside their scope in an Exclusive Scope of Practice State?
A) A written warning only
B) Monetary fines and jail time
C) Loss of NANP membership only
D) Mandatory continuing education
Correct Answer: B) Monetary fines and jail time
Rationale: NANP cautions that practicing outside the legal scope in these states can bring both fines and jail time. This is why understanding state law is a core professional duty.
Reference: NANP, Legislative Affairs (Exclusive Scope of Practice States)
33. What is NANP's guidance on whether HNPs can order laboratory assessments?
A) HNPs may never order lab tests
B) HNPs may order any lab test in every state
C) HNPs may be able to order laboratory assessments, so they should check the laws in their state
D) Only BCHN® holders may order lab tests
Correct Answer: C) HNPs may be able to order laboratory assessments, so they should check the laws in their state
Rationale: Lab-ordering rights depend on state law. NANP directs practitioners to verify their own state's rules rather than assume.
Reference: NANP, Legislative Affairs (Scope of Practice)
34. An HNP in a restrictive state gives a community talk and works in a health food store. What should the HNP always state?
A) That they are not a dietitian or nutritionist and are not licensed or certified by the state
B) That their advice replaces medical care
C) That they are an NANP Board member
D) That their recommendations are individualized
Correct Answer: A) That they are not a dietitian or nutritionist and are not licensed or certified by the state
Rationale: NANP notes these settings are often allowed, but the practitioner must make clear they hold no protected title and no state license or certification.
Reference: NANP, Legislative Affairs (A Note About General Nutrition Information)
35. Where in a state's law does the "general nutrition information" clause typically appear?
A) In the definitions of dietetic practice
B) In the licensing fee schedule
C) In the continuing education requirements
D) In the Exemptions or Exceptions section
Correct Answer: D) In the Exemptions or Exceptions section
Rationale: NANP explains that every state, including Exclusive Scope of Practice States, has a general nutrition information exemption. It usually sits in the law's Exemptions or Exceptions section.
Reference: NANP, Legislative Affairs (A Note About General Nutrition Information)
36. Which abbreviation is NOT among those NANP lists as protected in Exclusive Scope of Practice States?
A) L.D.
B) BCHN
C) R.D.N.
D) C.N.S.
Correct Answer: B) BCHN
Rationale: NANP lists L.D., L.N., R.D.N. and C.N.S. as protected abbreviations, along with titles such as dietitian and nutritionist. BCHN® is NANP's own registered credential, not a state-protected title.
Reference: NANP, Legislative Affairs (Exclusive Scope of Practice States)
Part 3: What Is Holistic Nutrition? (Q37–47)
37. The philosophy of holistic nutrition holds that a person's health is an expression of the complex interplay among which aspects of life?
A) Genetic, hormonal and metabolic factors only
B) Diet, exercise and sleep only
C) Physical and chemical, mental and emotional, and spiritual and environmental aspects
D) Medical history, medications and lab values
Correct Answer: C) Physical and chemical, mental and emotional, and spiritual and environmental aspects
Rationale: NANP defines holistic nutrition as a whole-person approach. Health reflects how all of these dimensions interact, not any single system.
Reference: NANP, What Is Holistic Nutrition? (philosophy)
38. According to NANP, what is the primary tool holistic nutrition professionals use to build health?
A) Nutritional education
B) Dietary supplement protocols
C) Laboratory testing
D) Elimination diets
Correct Answer: A) Nutritional education
Rationale: NANP states that HNPs use nutritional education as their primary tool, approaching each person as a unique individual. Supplements and labs may play a role but are not the primary tool.
Reference: NANP, What Is Holistic Nutrition? (philosophy)
39. NANP says the overlap in scope of practice between health coaches and holistic nutrition professionals is limited to:
A) Meal planning
B) Supplement recommendations
C) Reviewing laboratory assessments
D) Motivational interviewing techniques
Correct Answer: D) Motivational interviewing techniques
Rationale: The shared ground is motivational interviewing. HNPs' extensive clinical education gives them a broader scope than health coaching.
Reference: NANP, What Is Holistic Nutrition? (Holistic Nutrition Professional vs. Health Coach)
40. Which state requires state certification, rather than a license, to use the title "nutritionist"?
A) Alaska
B) Virginia
C) Minnesota
D) Louisiana
Correct Answer: B) Virginia
Rationale: NANP lists Alaska, the District of Columbia, Louisiana, Minnesota and New Mexico as requiring a license to use "nutritionist." Virginia instead requires state certification.
Reference: NANP, What Is Holistic Nutrition? ("Nutrition Professional" vs. "Nutritionist")
41. Why does NANP use the term "Nutrition Professional" instead of "Nutritionist"?
A) "Nutritionist" refers only to dietitians nationwide
B) Federal law prohibits the word "nutritionist"
C) Several states restrict the title "nutritionist," and "Nutrition Professional" covers all careers in the field
D) "Nutrition Professional" is a state-licensed title
Correct Answer: C) Several states restrict the title "nutritionist," and "Nutrition Professional" covers all careers in the field
Rationale: Because of title restrictions, NANP chose an umbrella term. It includes roles such as Nutrition Educator, Nutrition Consultant and Natural Chef.
Reference: NANP, What Is Holistic Nutrition? ("Nutrition Professional" vs. "Nutritionist")
42. When working with a licensed healthcare provider, a holistic nutrition professional may help individuals with previously diagnosed illnesses to:
A) Identify biochemical imbalances and toxicities that contribute to poor health
B) Diagnose additional conditions
C) Adjust prescription medications
D) Manage the disease state independently
Correct Answer: A) Identify biochemical imbalances and toxicities that contribute to poor health
Rationale: NANP describes this collaborative role alongside a licensed provider. Diagnosing, prescribing and managing disease remain outside HNP scope.
Reference: NANP, What Is Holistic Nutrition? (What Does a Holistic Nutrition Professional Do?)
43. Which of the following appears on NANP's role delineation list of things holistic nutrition professionals do NOT do?
A) Promote active lifestyles
B) Teach healthy eating
C) Educate the public on the benefits of unprocessed, local, fresh, plant-based foods
D) Use network marketing or product sales in lieu of individual client education
Correct Answer: D) Use network marketing or product sales in lieu of individual client education
Rationale: The "do not" list also includes practicing medical nutrition, diagnosing and treating disease, making unproven health claims, misrepresenting training, and using unproven devices, assessments or therapies. Promoting active lifestyles and teaching healthy eating are listed as things HNPs do.
Reference: NANP, What Is Holistic Nutrition? (Role Delineation)
44. Which is one of NANP's listed distinguishing features of holistic nutrition professionals?
A) Product-based programs built around a signature supplement line
B) Client-based, not method or product-based
C) Treats medical conditions without physician involvement
D) Relies on a single standardized protocol for all clients
Correct Answer: B) Client-based, not method or product-based
Rationale: Other listed features include a fresh, natural-foods basis, an educational emphasis, referral to doctors for medical conditions, and use of informed consent and full disclosure forms.
Reference: NANP, What Is Holistic Nutrition? (Distinguishing Features)
45. Which service does NANP list among the therapeutic services an HNP might offer, rather than the typical services?
A) Shopping tours
B) Family meal planning
C) Reviewing laboratory assessments
D) Teaching healthy eating
Correct Answer: C) Reviewing laboratory assessments
Rationale: Therapeutic services include intake, advising therapeutic menus and nutrients, reviewing lab assessments and consulting with a licensed practitioner. Shopping tours, family meal planning and teaching healthy eating are typical services.
Reference: NANP, What Is Holistic Nutrition? (Services)
46. How does NANP describe the role of a Natural Chef?
A) Teaches clients and groups to eat for health and provides healthy meals for them
B) Develops medical nutrition therapy plans for hospital patients
C) Sells supplements alongside prepared meals
D) Reviews laboratory assessments to design menus
Correct Answer: A) Teaches clients and groups to eat for health and provides healthy meals for them
Rationale: The Natural Chef combines nutrition education with preparing healthy meals. It is one of the careers covered by the term "Nutrition Professional."
Reference: NANP, What Is Holistic Nutrition? (What Does a Holistic Nutrition Professional Do?)
47. According to NANP, how do holistic nutrition professionals approach their working relationship with clients?
A) By prescribing an expert-led protocol the client follows without input
B) By using the same standardized program for every client
C) By leading with product recommendations
D) By fully engaging the individual and honoring their innate wisdom in an empowering, cooperative way
Correct Answer: D) By fully engaging the individual and honoring their innate wisdom in an empowering, cooperative way
Rationale: NANP describes treating each person as unique and partnering with them to chart a course toward optimal health. This reflects the holistic, client-centered philosophy.
Reference: NANP, What Is Holistic Nutrition? (philosophy)
Part 4: Additional questions (Q48–50)
48. A graduate of an NANP-approved holistic nutrition program, who has chosen not to join NANP, contacts NANP Legislative Affairs about her right to practice in her state. What does NANP advise?
A) Legislative Affairs will advise any graduate of an approved program, member or not
B) She should consult her personal attorney, because non-members have not gone through NANP's member vetting or agreed to its ethics and scope of practice policies
C) She should contact her state's dietetics licensing board for a free consultation
D) She may rely on the interactive state map as formal legal advice
Correct Answer: B) She should consult her personal attorney, because non-members have not gone through NANP's member vetting or agreed to its ethics and scope of practice policies
Rationale: NANP explains that advising unvetted non-members would put its credibility and standards at risk. NANP also states that the information on its website is not legal advice.
Reference: NANP, Legislative Affairs (membership and legal disclaimer)
49. An HNP relocates to Arizona, which NANP lists as a No Law or Certification State. Which statement best reflects NANP's guidance for this practitioner?
A) The state places no regulatory limits on who may give nutrition advice, but some such states protect specific titles and new regulations can be added, so she should check the state's laws
B) She may use any title, including "dietitian," because the state has no nutrition law
C) She must obtain a state certification before advising individual clients
D) She may provide only general nutrition information, never individualized recommendations
Correct Answer: A) The state places no regulatory limits on who may give nutrition advice, but some such states protect specific titles and new regulations can be added, so she should check the state's laws
Rationale: No Law or Certification States are the most lenient category. NANP still cautions that title protections may exist and that laws change, so ongoing review is required.
Reference: NANP, Legislative Affairs (No Law or Certification States)
50. According to NANP, how might a licensed healthcare provider such as an MD, ND, DO, DC or RN relate to the services of a holistic nutrition professional?
A) The provider must formally supervise every HNP consultation
B) The provider may work with an HNP to educate patients on whole foods, lifestyle improvements and appropriate supplementation, or may provide these services themselves
C) The provider is legally barred from offering nutrition education
D) The provider may delegate diagnosis and prescribing to the HNP
Correct Answer: B) The provider may work with an HNP to educate patients on whole foods, lifestyle improvements and appropriate supplementation, or may provide these services themselves
Rationale: NANP describes a collaborative, optional relationship. Diagnosis and prescribing stay with the licensed provider and outside HNP scope.
Reference: NANP, What Is Holistic Nutrition? (What Does a Holistic Nutrition Professional Do?)
