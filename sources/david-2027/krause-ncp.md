**Question 1** 

In clinical nutrition assessment, the gastrointestinal tract is evaluated both as a digestive organ and a major immune organ. Which feature best reflects this dual role? 

A) The stomach contains 80% of the body's immune cells and absorbs the majority of micronutrients. 

B) The GI tract houses the highest concentration of immune cells in the body and utilizes epithelial cells (3–5 day lifespan) to maintain barrier and immune defense functions. 

C) Parietal cells act as antigen-presenting cells that clear pathogens through active transport. 

D) Salivary amylase coordinates systemic antigen responses while digesting trace minerals.

**Correct Answer: B) The GI tract houses the highest concentration of immune cells in the body and utilizes epithelial cells (3–5 day lifespan) to maintain barrier and immune defense functions.**

**Rationale:** Gut-associated lymphoid tissue (GALT) represents the largest compartment of immune cells in the human body. Supported by an epithelial layer with a rapid 3–5 day cell cycle, the mucosal barrier prevents pathogen translocation while coordinating immune tolerance and antigen recognition.

**Textbook Reference:** Raymond & Morrow, *Krause and Mahan's Food & the Nutrition Care Process*, Chapter 1: Intake: Gastrointestinal Digestion, Absorption, and Excretion of Nutrients (Section: The Gastrointestinal Tract / Mucosal Integrity & Immune Protection).

**Question 2** Which gut peptide hormone is primarily synthesized in the stomach to communicate hunger signals to the brain? A) Secretin B) Gastrin C) Ghrelin D) Pepsinogen

* **Correct Answer: C) Ghrelin**  
* **Rationale:** Ghrelin is an orexigenic peptide hormone synthesized predominantly in the stomach mucosa. It signals central appetite centers in the brain to trigger feelings of hunger and signal meal initiation.  
* **Textbook Reference:** Raymond & Morrow, *Krause and Mahan's Food & the Nutrition Care Process*, Chapter 1: Intake: Gastrointestinal Digestion, Absorption, and Excretion of Nutrients (Section: Hormonal Regulation of Appetite & Digestion).

**Question 3** During a clinical evaluation of stomach digestion, a practitioner considers how gastric secretions are regulated. What is the main function of the hormone gastrin? **Question 4** Why is pepsin secreted into the gastric lumen as pepsinogen rather than in its fully active form? B) Storing and secreting pepsinogen prevents autodigestion of the chief cells and gastric tissue prior to acid activation. * **Correct Answer: B) Storing and secreting pepsinogen prevents autodigestion of the chief cells and gastric tissue prior to acid activation.**  
* **Rationale:** Synthesizing and storing proteolytic enzymes as inactive zymogens (pepsinogen) protects mucosal glands and gastric tissue against self-digestion. Activation occurs safely in the luminal space once mixed with hydrochloric acid.

**Question 9** If a client experiences carbohydrate malabsorption due to blunting of the small intestinal villi, which enzymes are most directly affected? A) Salivary amylase and gastric pepsin B) Brush border disaccharidases, including sucrase, lactase, and maltase C) Pancreatic secretin and gastric ghrelin D) Parietal cell hydrochloric acid and gastrin

**Question 10** When formulating a dietary supplement to optimize mineral absorption, why might a manufacturer chelate trace minerals with organic acids or amino acids? Chelating the mineral with organic ligands (amino acids or organic acids) preserves solubility and neutralizes charge, optimizing uptake across the intestinal epithelium.

### **Question 1**

A student is reviewing energy terminology. Which of the following statements correctly distinguishes between Basal Energy Expenditure (BEE) and Total Energy Expenditure (TEE)?

A) BEE includes physical activity thermogenesis, whereas TEE accounts only for sleeping metabolic rate.

B) BEE is the baseline minimal energy needed for vital organ function, whereas TEE is the sum of BEE, the thermic effect of food, and activity thermogenesis.

C) TEE is measured immediately upon waking after an overnight fast, while BEE is measured after consuming a standard liquid test meal.

D) BEE and TEE are identical values in individuals who maintain a sedentary lifestyle.

**Correct Answer:** B) BEE is the baseline minimal energy needed for vital organ function, whereas TEE is the sum of BEE, the thermic effect of food, and activity thermogenesis.

**Rationale:** BEE (or BMR) is the lowest level of energy expenditure necessary to maintain cell and organ viability in a resting, postabsorptive state. TEE expands upon BEE by adding the energy costs of digesting food (TEF) and daily bodily movements/exercise (AT).

**Textbook Reference:** Raymond & Morrow, *Krause and Mahan's Food & the Nutrition Care Process*, Chapter 2: Energy.
